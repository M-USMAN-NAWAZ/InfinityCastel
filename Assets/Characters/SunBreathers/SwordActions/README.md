# Tanjiro sword draw and sheathe

The approved one-handed running/sprinting carry is enabled in gameplay. `TanjiroSword.enableSwordLocomotion` defaults to true on both Tanjiro gameplay prefabs, and Playground inherits it without a scene override. It activates while the sword is drawn; sheathed movement keeps the original gait. Draw and sheathe remain enabled.

Press **R** to draw the katana and **R** again to sheathe it. Mobile castle controls get a **DRAW / SHEATHE** button beside the upper jump controls. `TanjiroSword.ToggleSword()` is also available for a UI event or future input binding. Repeated presses during a transfer are ignored.

The existing Playground player inherits `TanjiroSword` from `Tanjiro.prefab`; no scene/player replacement is needed. `TanjiroThirdPerson.prefab` also includes it. At runtime it selects `TanjiroSword.controller`, a copy of the existing Starter Assets locomotion controller plus the masked **Sword Actions** layer. The original Starter Assets controller is unchanged.

## Animation assets

- `DrawKatana.anim`: 3-second anticipation, grip, extraction and ready sequence at 24 FPS, following the 12 reference beats with continuous interpolation.
- `SheatheKatana.anim`: 3-second return, insertion, release and settle sequence at 24 FPS.
- `KatanaReady.anim`: loopable held-sword upper-body pose.
- `KatanaRun.anim` / `KatanaSprint.anim`: 24 FPS right-handed waist carries based on the latest video, with the blade forward/slightly down and stronger forward lean while sprinting. The right-arm-only Sword Locomotion layer selects run/sprint using `SwordStride` at 1/2.
- `SwordRunningRight.mask` / `SwordFreeArm.mask`: right-arm/torso carry and left-arm/fingers original-gait masks. Sword Free Arm is natively synchronized to the base layer and explicitly reuses its motions; no duplicate Animator is used in gameplay.
- `SwordUpperBody.mask`: upper-body/arms/fingers, without legs or root motion.

These are Unity Humanoid muscle clips authored against Tanjiro's shared rig. The editor bake uses bounded, temporally continuous muscle poses; Unity's native hand IK keeps the grip and sheath aligned during playback. A hidden, zero-duration initialization solve calibrates the imported hand-bone axes to Unity's IK goal axes. The two hands have mirrored grip frames and independent handle-roll angles, rather than forcing the same palm orientation onto both wrists. The right hand holds immediately behind the guard; the left guides the sheath before blending onto the lower handle once the blade is clear. No new animation package is required. Existing walking, jumping and gravity remain controller-driven. Animation events move one sword between `KatanaGrip` and `KatanaSheath`; disabling the component resets it to the sheath.

The katana uses the supplied Yoriichi geometry/material/inscription, normalized out of the FBX's internal centimetres and fitted to Tanjiro's proportions at 0.58 scale. The stowed view contains only its hilt/collar; the full blade is shown during extraction and while held. A separate low-poly black-lacquer sheath with a gold mouth follows the guiding hand during extraction and returns to the hip afterward.

The ready pose preserves the upright locomotion torso and aims the blade forward on the character's centre line. The 28-degree diagonal palm grip is applied only to the hand socket, not to the sword's aiming axis. Both elbows settle into the guard without a shoulder shrug. The hand reaches forward/up away from the hip before crossing the jacket; the same arc is reversed on sheathing. Extraction follows a monotonic smooth curve through the reference beats rather than stopping at every beat. The right hand remains next to the guard, with the left below it. Each action remains three seconds.

`TanjiroDrawableBody.asset` is a separate skinned mesh with the original fixed hilt and sheath removed and their boundaries closed with skinned uniform faces. The original character FBX, textures and body mesh are not overwritten. `DrawableKatana.asset` and `StowedHilt.asset` preserve the sword UVs and share its existing material.

## Rebuild and validation

Run `Tools/RunArchitectureValidation.ps1 -Mode SwordAnimation` in the project root. It builds/tests in `Temp/ArchitectureValidation` and copies only the generated sword-action assets and the two updated Tanjiro prefabs back; it does not overwrite Playground.

`Temp/sword-animation-tests.txt` checks Humanoid muscle limits, arm/wrist continuity at 24 and 96 FPS, physical blade/sheath contact, root/leg isolation, sword units, attachment events, repeated requests, disable/reset, walking during the draw, normal jumping, sideways gravity and inheritance on the existing Playground player. It also checks the centred blade, upright torso, elbow height, each hand's position along/across the handle, grip angle and wrist bend (right below 28 degrees; left below 40 degrees). Grip/early-extraction right-wrist bend must stay below 60 degrees. Numerical results are recorded in that report rather than copied here.

The reach/return clearance check samples wrist, palm, knuckle and fingertip spheres against a posed, torso-weighted mesh surface at 96 FPS. These are editor-only diagnostic colliders; no collision-checking objects or added per-frame queries are installed on the gameplay prefabs.

For isolated front/side review in the open editor, call `TanjiroSwordReview.Run(false, true)`. It creates and cleans up a temporary additive scene without saving or replacing Playground. Images and a separate 96 FPS continuity report for both actions are written to `Temp/sword-pro`. Encode its 144 fixed-camera frames with Blender using `Tools/render_sword_animation_preview.py` to produce `SourceArt/Characters/Tanjiro-SwordActions-Polished24fps.mp4` (6 seconds, 24 FPS). Earlier preview files are retained but do not show this revision.

The phone button uses the existing UI canvas and look-input blocking. Actual phone interaction and device frame rate still require device testing; these tests run in Unity's desktop editor.

Drawn-sword running uses the third-person motor's smoothed `Speed` relative to its actual MoveSpeed/SprintSpeed, not a new movement control. Keyboard and mobile movement use the same path. Airborne movement returns toward the normal guard. The player root, capsule, camera pivot and leg gait are not tilted by the sword layer. Stopping blends back to the existing upright guard. A sheathe request while running first returns the trailing blade to guard before starting reinsertion.

For the one-handed carry, the right fixed-length analytic arm chain runs after Animator evaluation, with an explicit bend plane. Its transition hint arcs around the outside of the shoulder instead of crossing the arm axis. This avoids elbow/twist flips as the hand moves between the forward guard and waist carry. The left arm is never solved or keyed by the carry; the synchronized masked layer restores its existing gait, including fingers. Draw/sheathing and the stationary guard retain their existing native IK. There are no physics queries, runtime pose searches or new packages.

The October 26 video replaces the raised two-handed preview: only the right hand holds the sword, carried back beside the right waist. The blade points forward and slightly down, outside the leg. The left hand follows the original run/fast-run animation. Run and sprint use stronger forward lean at sprint speed. The original hand socket, sword appearance/proportions, draw, sheathing and stationary ready pose are unchanged. The approved forward-elbow revision is enabled in the gameplay prefabs.

The sword-side upper arm counter-swings from the shoulder, driven by the actual free-arm gait rather than a separate timer. The checked, lowered elbow is the furthest-back position: the cycle moves forward/down from it, never up/back beyond it. The run has a forward bias to compensate for its more upright torso. Grip and elbow targets rotate together around the shoulder; 20% wrist pitch compensation keeps the blade forward/slightly down. Both carries use the same relaxed palm roll to reduce forearm twisting and smooth speed changes. The free arm is unchanged. The Inspector toggle can still disable the carry if needed.

Run `TanjiroSwordReview.Run(false, true, true)` for the six-second idle/run/sprint/run/stop preview in `Temp/sword-run`. Encode with `Tools/render_sword_animation_preview.py --run` through Blender (arguments after `--`) to produce `SourceArt/Characters/Tanjiro-SwordRunning-ForwardElbow24fps.mp4`. Earlier videos are retained but do not show this revision. The isolated SwordAnimation test checks the enabled gameplay carry and its optional off switch, two looping clips, no left-arm/left-IK keys, speed selection, one-handed grip/blade direction, forward lean, grip/wrist stability at 96 FPS, forearm/upper-arm rotation, original socket preservation, stopping and sprint-to-sheathe completion. Both gaits must show at least 6 cm of forward elbow travel, stay within the checked rear depth (20.5 cm behind the shoulder in torso space), and keep the elbow at least 3.5 cm below the shoulder. The editor review checks these same bounds, compares the free arm's shoulder/upper arm/forearm/hand/finger local rotations to the unmodified base gait, and checks transitions and blade/character intersections across every sampled frame. Its hidden baseline actor and diagnostic colliders exist only in the temporary review scene, never in gameplay.
