# Infinity Castle

Open the Starter Assets `Playground` scene, stop an existing Play session, and start Play again. The new pools need a fresh session.

The runtime uses the assigned Danish buildings at their original scale. Unit-scale module parents position the art; they do not resize it. `stair_1` is the level walkway, and `stair_3` supplies the stepped flights. Timber balconies, fitted stair collision wedges, and connector landings provide continuous walking surfaces.

The runtime prefab's `Architecture Finishes` restore the unassigned imported albedo textures on shared runtime materials. Timber, paper panels, and illuminated window finishes are editable there; the original model and material assets are not modified.

## Controls

- WASD: camera-relative movement.
- Space: jump, including sideways and inverted gravity.
- Shift: sprint.
- Mouse: look.
- Android: drag any free screen area to look; UI retains its touches. The movement joystick, jump, and sprint work independently.

The native CharacterController supplies capsule dimensions but is disabled during play. InfinityCharacterMotor uses PhysX capsule sweeps in the current gravity frame. ThirdPersonController owns movement and jump velocity; InfinityGravityBody only selects gravity. There is no second world-down gravity pass.

## Castle Rules

- Building, connector, and landing pools are populated once during initialization. Streaming does not instantiate or destroy modules.
- The default `Random Nearby Castle` layout grows an irregular graph of base balconies, walls, and ceilings around the player. It does not build the old foreground floor grid. The spatial budget extends in all six directions.
- Connected routes are placed before decorative branches. Neighboring playable buildings have continuous walking connections using the original stair and walkway art, varied heights, and irregular lateral offsets. Loops reconnect existing neighbors. Jumping is optional, not the only way to travel.
- Gravity corners use short timber landings rather than stairs between incompatible gravity frames. Their turn faces include a support lip, and corner-only contact alignment keeps the capsule on the front of the next face. Corner ports are offset from ordinary stair ports so their gravity fields do not compete. Capsule-center proximity selects the closest surface with hysteresis. Walking onto a corner changes gravity, including the floor-to-wall-to-ceiling route near the starting balcony. Gravity persists across airborne gaps until another surface takes over.
- Gameplay entrances use base balconies: the authored buildings rise above their platforms in their own gravity frame. The automatic reverse-gravity undersides remain inactive. Gravity corners reuse correctly oriented existing balconies when possible, otherwise add pooled buildings with the required orientation. Occupied balconies request same-gravity walking exits using four candidate attempts per frame, including wider offsets when compact routes are blocked.
- Retained modules keep their world-space assignments as the player travels. Only outgoing modules are pooled, and free frontier positions are filled with them. Falling and travel upward, backward, or sideways use the same spatial streaming rules.
- The Castle Director waits 4-7 quiet seconds initially, then 7-13 quiet seconds between one or two one-way shifts. It waits for motion to settle before counting the next interval. These commands select close playable buildings, not distant decoration. Connected buildings slide within their gravity plane and may turn when clear; stairs retain their authored rise and their landings follow the endpoints. Occupied connectors stay protected. Buildings do not disappear simply because a shift finished.
- Periodic rearrangement waits 25-40 quiet seconds by default, then plans a castle-wide wave of visible translations and rotations. The same active modules move continuously between poses: no fading, retirement, hidden rotation, teleportation, or replacement is part of the cycle. Buildings remain at their original scale. Rotation arcs, not just final poses, are reserved before movement.
- Connected wings of two to four buildings are considered before independent motion. A wing slides up to six metres in its gravity plane with one shared trajectory; its internal floors and stairs keep their relative positions. Boundary stairs must also have clearance throughout the movement. Each wing is accepted atomically, and approaching any member stops the whole wing in its current pose. If no shared route is clear, independent clearance-checked motion remains available.
- Whole-castle planning considers adjacent architecture first. Connected playable buildings slide in their gravity plane and may yaw only when their buildings, headroom, and attached connection sweeps have clearance. Stair endpoints follow the actual rotated roof edges while retaining the authored stair rise. Unoccupied gravity-corner walls and ceilings can slide within their own gravity plane, including vertical wall movement; both corner landings and their gravity volumes follow the moving faces. Distant buildings can also tilt into a different gravity orientation; their gravity direction follows their visible rotation. Approaching space, occupied connectors, an escape route and predicted landing support stay protected. Blocked buildings retry after other pieces settle, up to three planning passes; genuinely blocked pieces remain still.
- `Nearby Shift Travel` defaults to 12 metres, independently of the distant-decoration clearance margin. Individual motion plans try successively smaller distances only when a larger sweep is blocked. `Ride Moving Buildings` allows the occupied building to slide while the motor carries the player by the support's actual frame displacement. Rider motion preserves the gravity frame and does not rotate the occupied floor. Unoccupied architecture can turn. Moving away from the deck's safe interior or jumping stops the occupied shift at its current pose. This does not cancel the remaining castle-wide wave. Rider shifts have an additional quiet cooldown.
- Approaching a moving building stops it safely at its current pose, without snapping it back, during both small shifts and whole-castle motion. A significant fall stops rearrangement, and predicted landing support remains protected. `Periodic Rebuilding`, `Rebuild Interval`, `Rebuild Move Seconds`, and `Rebuild Travel` control the cycle. Diagnostic counters report selected, protected, moved, rotated, blocked, near-field and gravity-corner modules. `NearFieldMotionCount` and `LastRebuildNearMovedCount` distinguish local physical movement from background changes.
- Building sweeps, connector sweeps, and four metres of playable headroom are reserved before movement. Motion is rejected if it intersects another building, a connector, or an approaching player's capsule. Only the carried player's own support can bypass the player exclusion, while its full headroom and connection sweeps remain checked. Solid faces pause if a non-rider enters them.
- Balcony gravity and attached stairs share the same orientation. No stair connects incompatible gravity frames.
- The starting route is world-up. Platforms sit just below each building's authored lower bound, so the tower stands on its base rather than hanging underneath it. Decorative balconies use the same base convention. Spawn and landing points use the open perimeter around the tower; stairs join perimeter ports. Gravity branches face outward around their corners to keep the tower bodies clear. Imported model rotations and scales remain unchanged; the containing building module is oriented to its gravity frame.
- Falling predicts the player's arrival and assigns an open perimeter point on a fixed pooled base balcony below that trajectory. The same random neighborhood grows around this building; there is no broad courtyard grid in the default mode. Prediction includes terminal fall speed. A supporting building is never recycled out from under its rider. The optional legacy layout retains its older courtyard landing districts.
- The assigned scene skybox is kept and rendered by the camera. No opaque enclosing box or terminal bottom sheet hides it. Optional castle lighting changes foreground light and fog only.

The layout is anime-inspired rather than a frame-for-frame recreation of the film. The imported buildings have large, differing bounds, so clearance determines spacing rather than scaling their geometry.

## Android

- Android is the first target: IL2CPP, ARM64, Input System, optimized frame pacing, landscape orientations, and a 30 FPS runtime target. A frame-rate target is not a measured device-performance guarantee.
- The default mobile profile prewarms 128 nearby buildings and 144 decorative buildings, with 256 connection slots. The default random layout does not allocate unused legacy landing districts. Four background layers remain distributed across all six directions. Emergency landings have priority over ordinary layout work.
- Growth and rearrangement planning run as resumable, count-limited work with a soft two-millisecond budget. Ordinary layout growth is suspended during rearrangement; emergency landings still have priority. Rearrangement never churns the pools. A single placement or motion plan can exceed the soft time budget; phone profiling is still required.
- Nine lightweight distant prefabs in `Assets/InfinityCastle/MobileArchitecture` share the exact original meshes, materials, bounds, and scale. They omit detailed mesh collision and shadow casting. Nearby playable buildings retain their authored collision. The `Infinity Castle/Bake Mobile Architecture` editor command regenerates these variants after artwork changes.
- The existing Starter Assets mobile canvas is activated and bound to the player on mobile only. The left joystick moves, and the existing buttons jump and sprint. The old look joystick is hidden. `InfinityScreenLook` places an invisible full-viewport drag surface behind UI, outside the safe-area wrapper: free areas on either side rotate the camera, UI-started touches never become camera touches, and moving a camera finger over UI pauses look without accumulating a jump. Look uses pixel displacement normalized by screen height, not a held joystick rate. Controls respect the screen safe area, reserve each finger to its own control, clamp movement output, and clear input on release, disable, or app interruption. Enable `Preview Touch Controls` on the castle for editor testing.
- Android uses the Mobile render pipeline with 80% render scale, no HDR, two additional lights per object, and half-resolution texture mipmaps. The assigned skybox remains unchanged. Footsteps and landing audio reuse the player's AudioSource instead of creating temporary GameObjects.
- `InfinityCastleMobileBake.BuildAndroid` builds a development APK from Playground in an isolated batch project. Do not invoke this test build against the open working editor.

## Verification

`Assets/Editor/InfinityCastleValidation.cs` checks six gravity directions, jumps, natural walking gravity transitions, multiple consecutive stair/walkway crossings beyond the starting neighborhood, route coverage, quiet intervals between shifts, moving-building clearance, directional landing, pool reuse, and the saved Playground configuration. Legacy grid connector traversal is checked when that optional layout is selected.

Run it in an isolated Unity project copy with `-batchmode -nographics -quit -executeMethod InfinityCastleValidation.Run`. Do not run validation against an open working scene. The validation entry point intentionally refuses non-batch execution.

`InfinityCastleValidation.RenderPlayground` runs an isolated play-mode preview, checks actual WASD/Space input and walking the original stair flight, and captures the actual scene before and during random shifts.
It also checks live touch movement, camera look, jumping and landing, captures landscape control layouts, captures actual close-range motion, waits for timed whole-castle motion, and compares the completed architecture from the same camera position. `InfinityCastleValidation.RunRebuilding` checks close playable shifts (including gravity corners), actual pose changes, moving corner landing gravity and clearance, rotation-sweep containment, adjacent motion in all six gravity frames, castle-wide selection and physical movement, visible rotation, per-frame pose continuity, coordinated-wing floor relationships and collective approach stopping, unchanged active module identity, protected support, stairs, pool reuse, fall safety, stationary timers, and idle allocations at a 30 FPS timestep. This is editor verification, not profiling on an Android device.

`InfinityCastleValidation.RunRiding` additionally checks screen-look ownership and UI exclusion logic, frame-normalized camera deltas, occupied platform carrying in all six gravity frames at a 30 FPS simulation timestep, building and moving-path clearance, large adjacent shifts, unchanged pool object count, and correctly oriented building bases in the saved Playground. The graphical preview checks actual rendered UI raycasts across free screen areas and control hit areas.

## Machine Learning

`InfinityCastleDirector` is a deterministic behavior controller, not a trained neural network. It observes motion and coordinates high-level changes; `DynamicInfinityCastle` owns clearance checks, pools, protected landings, and physical connections.

A future ML-Agents policy could choose shift direction, district style, group size, and timing through the same commands. Training would need observations, reward criteria, many simulation episodes, and a validated exported model. A learned policy must not bypass physical clearance or landing guarantees. No ML package, training run, or trained model is included in this implementation.
