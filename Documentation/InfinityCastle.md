# Infinity Castle

Open the Starter Assets `Playground` scene, stop an existing Play session, and start Play again. The new pools need a fresh session.

The default runtime uses CorrectedRoom, HallWayWithTurn1, HallWay3Way, DoubleStaresBase and SingleStareBase for nearby traversal. Walk on native building floors and roofs, enter adjoining rooms, and use a few narrow stair bridges or intentional jump gaps. Connected buildings and halls physically shift together. The original Danish towers remain distant architecture at their authored scale. Broad perimeter walkways and connector landings are not generated in this mode. See [Playable Building Architecture](SuppliedArchitecture.md) for the current topology and test commands.

All castle models use a unified dark-timber, warm-shoji and aged-brass finish. Shared grain and paper textures are in `Assets/InfinityCastle/CastleTheme`. The runtime prefab's `Architecture Finishes` preserve imported albedo detail and select each surface family. Character and skybox materials are not modified.

## Controls

- WASD: camera-relative movement.
- Space: jump, including sideways and inverted gravity.
- Shift: sprint.
- Mouse: look.
- Android: drag any free screen area to look; UI retains its touches. The movement joystick, jump, and sprint work independently.

The native CharacterController supplies capsule dimensions but is disabled during play. InfinityCharacterMotor uses PhysX capsule sweeps in the current gravity frame. ThirdPersonController owns movement and jump velocity; InfinityGravityBody only selects gravity. There is no second world-down gravity pass.

Gravity changes keep physics aligned immediately, while the camera eases into its new orientation and follow point over 0.9 seconds. `Gravity Camera Transition Duration` on ThirdPersonController controls this duration. Quaternion interpolation uses smooth easing, the Cinemachine world-up reference follows the same frame, and look input remains responsive. A second gravity change starts from the currently displayed pose instead of restarting from an old orientation.

## Legacy Castle Rules

The graph-specific rules below describe the optional older mode with `Use Building Traversal` disabled. The new default building graph is documented in `SuppliedArchitecture.md`. Controls, smooth camera changes, background density and the preserved skybox also apply to the new mode.

- Building, connector, and landing pools are populated once during initialization. Streaming does not instantiate or destroy modules.
- The default `Random Nearby Castle` layout grows an irregular graph of perimeter balconies, walls, and ceilings around the player. It does not build the old foreground floor grid. The spatial budget extends in all six directions.
- Connected walking routes are placed before optional branches. Attached courts use broad timber seams, while separated buildings use the original stairs and walkways with varied heights and irregular lateral offsets. Optional 1.0-2.2 metre jump gaps stay unbridged, and distant architecture can remain unreachable. Two walking exits are prioritized where space permits, with selective loops rather than a fully connected grid.
- Gravity corners use short timber landings rather than stairs between incompatible gravity frames. Their turn faces include a support lip, and corner-only contact alignment keeps the capsule on the front of the next face. Corner ports are offset from ordinary stair ports so their gravity fields do not compete. Capsule-center proximity selects the closest surface with hysteresis. Walking onto a corner changes gravity, including the floor-to-wall-to-ceiling route near the starting balcony. Gravity persists across airborne gaps until another surface takes over.
- Gravity-field distance uses the physical walkway strips and corner landing, not the empty centre or expanded trigger box. Corner joints require a shared lateral band and stay orthogonal; the outgoing turn face owns overlapping gravity fields through the transition. Buildings with attached gravity corners translate without yaw so these joints stay traversable during rearrangement.
- Gameplay entrances use open perimeter balconies: four physical timber strips leave the authored building footprint open, instead of adding a square base sheet. The authored buildings rise in their own gravity frame. The automatic reverse-gravity undersides remain inactive. Gravity corners reuse correctly oriented existing balconies when possible, otherwise add pooled buildings with the required orientation. Occupied balconies request same-gravity walking exits using four candidate attempts per frame, including wider offsets when compact routes are blocked.
- `Dense Distant Architecture` uses independent horizontal and vertical spacing measured from the original model footprints and heights. The default artwork gives approximately 35-metre horizontal and 75-metre vertical spacing, rather than 97 metres on every axis. Staggered storeys and cell jitter break up straight rows. Candidates fill from inner layers outward; a blocked slot does not consume the pool budget. Retained buildings keep their assignments while only outgoing cells are recycled. `Distant Building Gap` sets packing clearance, and `Decoration Pool Size` caps the background object count.
- Retained modules keep their world-space assignments as the player travels. Only outgoing modules are pooled, and free frontier positions are filled with them. Falling and travel upward, backward, or sideways use the same spatial streaming rules.
- The Castle Director waits 4-7 quiet seconds initially, then a base 7-13 quiet seconds between one or two one-way shifts. The learned tempo scales later intervals by 0.85-1.3. It waits for motion to settle before counting the next interval. These commands select close playable buildings, not distant decoration. Connected buildings slide within their gravity plane and may turn when clear; stairs retain their authored rise and their landings follow the endpoints. Occupied connectors stay protected. Buildings do not disappear simply because a shift finished.
- Periodic rearrangement waits 25-40 quiet seconds by default, then plans a castle-wide wave of visible translations and rotations. The same active modules move continuously between poses: no fading, retirement, hidden rotation, teleportation, or replacement is part of the cycle. Buildings remain at their original scale. Rotation arcs, not just final poses, are reserved before movement.
- Connected wings of two to four buildings are considered before independent motion. A wing slides up to six metres in its gravity plane with one shared trajectory; its internal floors and stairs keep their relative positions. Boundary stairs must also have clearance throughout the movement. Each wing is accepted atomically, and approaching any member stops the whole wing in its current pose. If no shared route is clear, independent clearance-checked motion remains available.
- Dense background banks of two to four neighboring towers can translate together by up to twelve metres when their full sweeps are clear. Shared motion preserves their internal separation, while unrelated architecture, playable headroom, stairs, and the player remain exclusions. Some towers are reserved for independent turns; smaller yaw and travel options keep dense areas animated when a quarter-turn cannot fit. Blocked pieces stay still rather than intersecting another mesh.
- Whole-castle planning considers adjacent architecture first. Connected playable buildings slide in their gravity plane and may yaw only when their buildings, headroom, and attached connection sweeps have clearance. Stair endpoints follow the actual rotated roof edges while retaining the authored stair rise. Unoccupied gravity-corner walls and ceilings can slide within their own gravity plane, including vertical wall movement; both corner landings and their gravity volumes follow the moving faces. Distant scenery can tilt when clear but has no gameplay gravity volumes; nearby playable gravity follows its building's visible orientation. Approaching space, occupied connectors, an escape route and predicted landing support stay protected. Blocked buildings retry after other pieces settle, up to three planning passes; genuinely blocked pieces remain still.
- `Nearby Shift Travel` defaults to 12 metres, independently of the distant-decoration clearance margin. Individual motion plans try successively smaller distances only when a larger sweep is blocked. `Ride Moving Buildings` allows the occupied building to slide while the motor carries the player by the support's actual frame displacement. Rider motion preserves the gravity frame and does not rotate the occupied floor. Unoccupied architecture can turn. Moving away from the deck's safe interior or jumping stops the occupied shift at its current pose. This does not cancel the remaining castle-wide wave. Rider shifts have an additional quiet cooldown.
- Approaching a moving building stops it safely at its current pose, without snapping it back, during both small shifts and whole-castle motion. A significant fall stops rearrangement, and predicted landing support remains protected. `Periodic Rebuilding`, `Rebuild Interval`, `Rebuild Move Seconds`, and `Rebuild Travel` control the cycle. Diagnostic counters report selected, protected, moved, rotated, blocked, near-field and gravity-corner modules. `NearFieldMotionCount` and `LastRebuildNearMovedCount` distinguish local physical movement from background changes.
- Building sweeps, connector sweeps, and four metres of playable headroom are reserved before movement. Motion is rejected if it intersects another building, a connector, or an approaching player's capsule. Only the carried player's own support can bypass the player exclusion, while its full headroom and connection sweeps remain checked. Solid faces pause if a non-rider enters them.
- Balcony gravity and attached stairs share the same orientation. No stair connects incompatible gravity frames.
- The starting route is world-up. Playable perimeter walkways sit just below each building's authored lower bound; background towers have only their authored floors and balconies. Spawn and landing points use the open perimeter around the tower; stairs join perimeter ports. Gravity branches face outward around their corners to keep the tower bodies clear. Imported model rotations and scales remain unchanged; the containing building module is oriented to its gravity frame.
- Falling predicts the player's arrival and assigns an open perimeter point on a fixed pooled balcony below that trajectory. The same random neighborhood grows around this building; there is no broad courtyard grid in the default mode. Prediction includes terminal fall speed. A supporting building is never recycled out from under its rider. The optional legacy layout retains its older courtyard landing districts.
- The assigned scene skybox is kept and rendered by the camera. No opaque enclosing box or terminal bottom sheet hides it. Optional castle lighting changes foreground light and fog only.

The layout is anime-inspired rather than a frame-for-frame recreation of the film. The imported buildings have large, differing bounds, so clearance determines spacing rather than scaling their geometry.

## Android

- Android is the first target: IL2CPP, ARM64, Input System, optimized frame pacing, landscape orientations, and a 30 FPS runtime target. A frame-rate target is not a measured device-performance guarantee.
- The mobile profile prewarms 20 playable building wings (approximately 121 room, corridor, rooftop and stair parts), 192 decorative towers, and eight narrow bridge slots. Two wings are reserved for fall recovery. Shared LODs reduce distant room geometry. No legacy landing districts or perimeter decks are allocated in building mode. Four background layers surround the player. Physical-phone profiling remains necessary before shipping.
- Growth and rearrangement planning run as resumable, count-limited work with a soft two-millisecond budget. Background work keeps its queue progress when nearby routes grow; only a change of background window restarts it. This prevents repeated blocked inner slots from starving distant refill after a fall. Ordinary layout growth is suspended during rearrangement; emergency landings still have priority. Rearrangement never churns the pools. A single placement or motion plan can exceed the soft time budget; phone profiling is still required.
- Nine lightweight distant prefabs in `Assets/InfinityCastle/MobileArchitecture` share the exact original meshes, materials, bounds, and scale. They omit detailed mesh collision and shadow casting. Nearby playable buildings retain their authored collision. The `Infinity Castle/Bake Mobile Architecture` editor command regenerates these variants after artwork changes.
- The existing Starter Assets mobile canvas is activated and bound to the player on mobile only. The left joystick moves, and the existing buttons jump and sprint. The old look joystick is hidden. `InfinityScreenLook` places an invisible full-viewport drag surface behind UI, outside the safe-area wrapper: free areas on either side rotate the camera, UI-started touches never become camera touches, and moving a camera finger over UI pauses look without accumulating a jump. Look uses pixel displacement normalized by screen height, not a held joystick rate. Controls respect the screen safe area, reserve each finger to its own control, clamp movement output, and clear input on release, disable, or app interruption. Enable `Preview Touch Controls` on the castle for editor testing.
- Android uses the Mobile render pipeline with 80% render scale, no HDR, two additional lights per object, and half-resolution texture mipmaps. The assigned skybox remains unchanged. Footsteps and landing audio reuse the player's AudioSource instead of creating temporary GameObjects.
- `InfinityCastleMobileBake.BuildAndroid` builds a development APK from Playground in an isolated batch project. Do not invoke this test build against the open working editor.

## Verification

The new default is checked by `InfinityCastleArchitectureValidation` and `Tools/RunArchitectureValidation.ps1`: actual interiors, roofs, enclosed corridors, sparse stairs, moving connected groups and six-gravity fall recovery without scene-object growth. The graph-specific checks below target legacy perimeter traversal.

`Assets/Editor/InfinityCastleValidation.cs` checks six gravity directions, jumps, natural walking gravity transitions, multiple consecutive stair/walkway crossings beyond the starting neighborhood, route coverage, quiet intervals between shifts, moving-building clearance, directional landing, pool reuse, and the saved Playground configuration. Legacy grid connector traversal is checked when that optional layout is selected.

Perimeter checks verify physical support and jumping on all four walkway strips in all six gravity frames, and ray-test that no floor fills the centre. Dense-layout checks verify the fixed background count, footprint-based spacing, and absence of added background base surfaces. The preview captures the denser architecture before and after a timed physical wave from the same inspection view.

`InfinityCastleValidation.RunCamera` provides focused 30/60 FPS checks for sideways and inverted camera transitions, the Cinemachine follow pipeline, smooth pivot settling, interrupted turns, and touch look during the blend. It also checks movement/jumping in all six gravity frames and full-screen mobile look ownership and UI exclusion.

Run it in an isolated Unity project copy with `-batchmode -nographics -quit -executeMethod InfinityCastleValidation.Run`. Do not run validation against an open working scene. The validation entry point intentionally refuses non-batch execution.

`InfinityCastleValidation.RenderPlayground` runs an isolated play-mode preview, checks actual WASD/Space input and walking the original stair flight, and captures the actual scene before and during random shifts.
It also checks live touch movement, camera look, jumping and landing, captures landscape control layouts, captures actual close-range motion, waits for timed whole-castle motion, and compares the completed architecture from the same camera position. `InfinityCastleValidation.RunRebuilding` checks close playable shifts (including gravity corners), actual pose changes, moving corner landing gravity and clearance, rotation-sweep containment, adjacent motion in all six gravity frames, castle-wide selection and physical movement, visible rotation, per-frame pose continuity, coordinated-wing floor relationships and collective approach stopping, unchanged active module identity, protected support, stairs, pool reuse, fall safety, stationary timers, and idle allocations at a 30 FPS timestep. This is editor verification, not profiling on an Android device.

`InfinityCastleValidation.RunRiding` additionally checks screen-look ownership and UI exclusion logic, frame-normalized camera deltas, occupied platform carrying in all six gravity frames at a 30 FPS simulation timestep, building and moving-path clearance, large adjacent shifts, unchanged pool object count, and correctly oriented building bases in the saved Playground. The graphical preview checks actual rendered UI raycasts across free screen areas and control hit areas.

## Machine Learning

Unity ML-Agents 4.0.3 now runs the trained `CastleDirector.onnx` policy through `InfinityCastleMLAgent`. It chooses layout archetype, motion phase, gravity-relative direction, and timing; `InfinityCastleDirector` executes those commands through the existing safety planner. `Use Learned Director` is enabled by default. The two most recent layouts are action-masked, with eight-decision frequency observations to discourage longer repeating cycles.

The included PPO baseline was trained for 151,552 high-level curriculum decisions. It runs locally on the Burst CPU backend only when a shift or rebuild is due. `DynamicInfinityCastle` remains authoritative for pooling, collision sweeps, connections, and protected landings. This is not full player-physics training or a model learned from anime footage. Training assets, reproduction steps, evaluation results, and limitations are in [CastleLearning.md](CastleLearning.md).
